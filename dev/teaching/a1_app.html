Appendix A — The allocation app

A.1 What it is

A Shiny app, provided with the course, that runs the allocation method of this book on the course dataset. It has four stations that follow Chapters 3 to 6. It does not connect to the game; you read numbers off it and type them into the game’s grids.

Source and installation: the course repository (2024_CESIM_Invest). It needs R with the packages shiny, DT, xts and quadprog; start it with shiny::runApp() in the app folder, or use the hosted instance your instructor names.

A.2 The data

  • data/classes_monthly.csv: the 21 positions, monthly total returns in US dollars, unhedged, with an index series; the full history the course uses. The strategic benchmark is defined on the 16 positions without the money market; the app’s default selection leaves the five money-market positions out, and they can be added for analysis.
  • data/riskfree_monthly.csv: the US money-market rate, monthly.
  • data/cap_shares.csv: the capitalisation shares of the five equity markets, for the home rule.

Positions are named <region>_<class>: US_money_market, UK_government, EU_credit, CH_equity, JP_equity, GLOBAL_commodity. Excess returns are total returns minus the money-market rate of the same month. Annualisation: mean × 12, covariance × 12.

A.3 Station 1 · Strategic Asset Analysis

Inputs: the positions (default the 16 benchmark positions), the part of the sample in percent of the observations (default all). Outputs: annualised statistics (excess return, volatility, Sharpe ratio, skewness, worst calendar year, maximum drawdown), the correlation matrix, the growth of 100.

A.4 Station 2 · Strategic Asset Allocation

Inputs: γ (whole numbers), home market and home share, objective (quadratic utility, minimum variance, maximum Sharpe), a label; floors and caps by asset class, by currency and per position. The problem solved:

\[ \max_w \; \mu' w - \frac{\gamma}{2}\, w' \Sigma w \quad \text{s.t.} \quad \sum w = 1,\; w \ge 0,\; \text{floors and caps},\; \text{home rule}, \]

with the home rule as one linear equality per foreign equity market: \(w_x = (1-h)\, s_x \sum_{\text{equity}} w\), where \(h\) is the home share and \(s_x\) the capitalisation share of market \(x\) outside the home market. Solved with quadprog::solve.QP(D = \gamma\Sigma, d = \mu, A, b, meq). Minimum variance sets μ = 0; maximum Sharpe is the best point of a sweep over γ. Weights are rounded to half a percent, the residual into the home equity position.

Outputs: the allocation as a currency × asset class matrix with sums, the positions with raw and rounded weights, class and currency totals, in-sample results of the fixed weights rebalanced monthly (expected excess return, volatility, Sharpe, best and worst calendar year, maximum drawdown), the binding limits, the in-sample path, the efficient frontier under the same limits, the constraint rows, and R code that reproduces the run. Fix this allocation as an SAA hands it to stations 3 and 4; several SAAs can be fixed, by label.

A.5 Station 3 · Tactical Analysis

Inputs: τ (default 0.05) and the views, typed one per line: US_equity 12 50 is an absolute view (US equities earn 12 % a year over cash, confidence 50 %); EU_equity > CH_equity 2 60 is a relative view (euro-area equities beat Swiss equities by 2 points a year, confidence 60 %). The confidence is optional and defaults to 50; lines starting with # are ignored; a list of position names is in the panel. The views apply to every fixed SAA; a selector chooses which SAA’s prior and posterior the table shows.

Prior: \(\Pi = \gamma \Sigma w_{\text{SAA}}\) (reverse optimisation with the SAA’s own γ and the unrounded weights). Posterior (He and Litterman 1999):

\[ \mu_{BL} = \left[(\tau\Sigma)^{-1} + P'\Omega^{-1}P\right]^{-1} \left[(\tau\Sigma)^{-1}\Pi + P'\Omega^{-1}Q\right], \qquad \Sigma_{BL} = \Sigma + \left[(\tau\Sigma)^{-1} + P'\Omega^{-1}P\right]^{-1}, \]

with \(\Omega\) diagonal and \(\Omega_k = \frac{1-c_k}{c_k}\,\tau\, p_k'\Sigma p_k\), so that c = 1/2 is the He–Litterman setting. Outputs: per position the SAA weight, the sample mean, the prior, the posterior and the change; the views as parsed.

A.6 Station 4 · Tactical Asset Allocation

Inputs: the maximum deviation per position (band, default 0.05) and the tracking-error budget (default 2 % a year, 0 = none). One press computes the tactical allocation of every fixed SAA under the same views. For each SAA, the problem of station 2 on \((\mu_{BL}, \Sigma)\) with that SAA’s γ and limits but without the home rule, plus \(w_{\text{SAA}} - d \le w \le w_{\text{SAA}} + d\). The home rule is left out because it defines the benchmark; kept, a view on one equity market could not act. The sample covariance Σ is used, not \(\Sigma_{BL}\): \(\Sigma_{BL}\) raises the measured risk by about \((1+\tau)\) as soon as any view is entered and moves weight from equities into bonds for no reason of the views. If a budget is set and the tracking error \(\sqrt{(w - w_{\text{SAA}})'\Sigma(w - w_{\text{SAA}})}\) exceeds it, the deviation is scaled to the budget. Outputs: a summary table with the tilt by asset class, tracking error and active return per fund, and a tab per fund with the SAA, TAA and deviation matrices, the active-position figures and the binding limits. A negative expected active return is flagged in both. Without views the TAA equals the SAA, and a view that agrees with the prior leaves it there.

A.7 Saving your work

Station 2 saves the fixed SAAs (their inputs and weights), the views and the tactical settings as a JSON file and loads such a file back. On loading, the SAAs are recomputed from their inputs on the current data, so a loaded file is always consistent with the dataset in use. Save the file in the team forum; it is the team’s record of the funds and views.

A.8 Tests

tests/check_recipe.R confirms that the app’s constraint builder and solver reproduce a hand-written quadratic programme for twenty client cases; tests/check_taa.R confirms that the tactical allocation equals the strategic one without views and under a view that agrees with the prior, that a negative view on one equity market lowers that market, and shows one view at work with a tracking-error budget.
