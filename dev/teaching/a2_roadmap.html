Appendix B — Proposed changes to the app and the game

Planning only. Nothing here is implemented; each item names what it would simplify in the flow of Chapters 2 to 9 and what it would take.

B.1 Simplifications of the flow

Station 0: the client. A form with the two decisions (σ and μ of each portfolio, taken or turned down) and the rules; the app computes the γ bracket, shows it, and carries the rules into station 2 as the limits. Removes the hand calculation of Chapter 2 and the manual entry of floors. Small.

Band check. Load the client’s guidance bands (from the course’s allocation workbook) and flag, in station 2 and station 4, any class outside its band. Removes the manual check of Chapters 4 and 6. Small; needs the bands in a machine-readable file in the app’s data/.

Game-format export. A button that writes the strategic or tactical matrix in the exact layout of the game’s grid (currency × asset class, percent) to the clipboard or a CSV. Removes typing errors. Small.

Outlook import. A paste field for the analyst’s segment estimates of the round (yields, spreads, expected excess market return, factor premia per currency) and a rule that turns them into candidate views against the prior, with the confidence derived from the expected range. Chapter 5 by hand becomes one paste. Medium; needs the game’s export format for the outlook.

All funds at once. Run stations 2 to 4 for the firm’s up to four funds in one pass: one views panel, a tab per fund, one combined export. Station 4’s “same views for every fixed SAA” table is the first step. Medium.

Round log. Keep, per fund and round, the SAA, the views, the TAA, the tracking error, and after the round the realised return and attribution (from the Results Excel), so that Chapter 9’s “judge the views” is a table rather than memory. Medium; needs the Results Excel format.

B.2 Extensions that implement further decisions

Security selection (Chapter 7). From the tactical segment weights to positions: value or equal weighting, an ESG floor, and the tracking error of the resulting stock book against the segment line; optionally overweights by factor loading or industry with their contribution to tracking error. Needs asset-level returns and ratings in the app’s data (ratings as levels, which the course dataset does not carry yet). Large.

Performance attribution (Chapter 9). Brinson–Fachler on the game’s Results Excel: allocation, selection and currency effects against the fixed SAA, per round and cumulative. Medium; the inputs are the round’s segment returns and weights.

Tracking-error optimisation proper. Replace the scaling of station 4 by an optimisation with the tracking error as a constraint (a second-order cone, or a search over the active risk aversion), so that the TAA at a budget is optimal rather than a shrunk optimum. Small to medium.

Estimation risk as a discussion tool. A toggle in station 2 that shows what shrinkage of the means toward the grand mean (or a Kan–Zhou-type combination) would do to the allocation, next to the plain optimum. Not for the benchmark, which stays the plain optimum; for the discussion of why a plain optimum is fragile and why the game defines the benchmark this way. Small.

Currency hedging as a hypothetical. Show what the allocation would be if foreign bonds could be hedged (local excess returns plus the interest differential, local covariance), next to the unhedged one the game allows. Makes Chapter 3’s point visible. Small; the hedged series would have to be added to the course dataset.

B.3 Suggestions for the game itself

Client texts and guidance bands. The October 2026 client profiles and guidance bands replace the current ones.

Strategic allocation analysis on the Analyst page. It compares the fund’s strategic allocation with the optimal allocation held in the game’s parameters. If it reports the distance exactly, a team can iterate to the hidden allocation round by round; a coarse report (inside the guidance band / outside, or a distance rounded to five points) keeps the client-reading exercise alive.

Tracking error on the Investment strategy page. It is estimated from the game’s own forecasts and a short history; the app’s figure uses the full history. Stating the horizon and the data behind the game’s estimate would make the two comparable.

Exports. A CSV export of the Economic outlook’s segment estimates and of the Results attribution would make the outlook import and the round log above possible.
